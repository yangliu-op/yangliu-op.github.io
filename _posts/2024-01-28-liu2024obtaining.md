Introduction
----
Consider the least-squares problem

$$
\min_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d} || \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{A} \mathbf{x} ||^2,
$$ 

where $ \mathbf{A} $ is symmetric. To solve it efficiently, one common approach is to use iterative solvers. Among them, MINRES is one of the most efficient solvers, with one matrix-vector multiplication per iteration. However, MINRES is not guaranteed to obtain the pseudoinverse solution $ \mathbf{x}^+ \triangleq \mathbf{A}^\dagger \mathbf{b} $ (also called the minimum-norm solution) when $ \mathbf{b} \not\in \text{Range}(\mathbf{A}) $.

Minimum-Norm Refinement
----
We propose a simple minimum-norm (MN) refinement to obtain $ \mathbf{x}^+ $, which is 

$$ 
\mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{x}_t - \frac{<\mathbf{r}_t, \mathbf{x}_t>}{|| \mathbf{r}_t ||^2} \mathbf{r}_t 
$$ 

where $ \mathbf{r}_t \triangleq \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{A} \mathbf{x}_t $ and $ t $ is the iteration counter. When MINRES terminates at $ t=g $, we must have $ \mathbf{y}_g = \mathbf{A}^\dagger \mathbf{b} $. (Note that the MN refinement can also be used in the conjugate residual (CR) method.)

PDE solving
----
We apply Nédélec finite elements to solve the following Maxwell problem 

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{curl} \text{ curl } \mathbf{u} &= \mathbf{f} \quad \text{in } \Omega \\
\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{n} &= 0 \quad \text{on } \partial \Omega
\end{aligned}
$$

with [Firedrake](https://www.firedrakeproject.org/) and [PETSc](https://petsc.org/release/manualpages/KSP/KSPMINRES/) using our MN refinement. We are particularly interested in this problem since it has infinitely many solutions for a general $ \mathbf{f} $, and we would like to find the minimum-norm solution among them using the MN refinement. Let us construct our problem in $ \mathbb{R}^2 $ with $ \mathbf{f} = \text{curl} \text{ curl } \mathbf{u}_{\text{exact}} + \mathbf{e} $, where $ \mathbf{e} $ is the projection of uniformly distributed random noise $ \mathcal{U}(-1, 1) $ onto the null space of the operator. 

The following recorded video shows that the MN refinement recovers $ \mathbf{u}_{\text{exact}} $ successfully. (From top to bottom: the MINRES iterates, and the corresponding MN refinement iterates. From left to right: the magnitude, the X-axis component, and the Y-axis component of the current iterate.)
